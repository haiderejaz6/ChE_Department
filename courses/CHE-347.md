# CHE-347: Chemical Reaction Engineering

| | |
|---|---|
| **Credit Hours** | 3-1 |
| **Prerequisites** | None |

## Course Objectives

- To provide in-depth knowledge of the application of the laws of thermodynamics and reaction kinetics for the economical design of chemical reactors.
- To develop skills in identifying the mechanism of a reaction.

## Course Contents

1. Introduction to reaction engineering and fundamentals of chemical reaction engineering.
2. Introduction to the types of reactions and rate of reaction.
3. Calculation of rates and rate expression representation for elementary & non-elementary reactions.
4. Model expressions for zeroth, 1st, 2nd, and 3rd order reactions. Half-life method and fractional-life method for evaluation of rate expressions.
5. Introduction to ideal reactor types.
6. Design and comprehensive parameters for ideal reactors.
7. Design for single reactions.
8. Best reactor set-up and contacting pattern for single reactions.
9. Product distribution for multiple reactions.
10. Design for complex multiple and solid-catalyzed reactions.
11. Best reactor set-up and contacting pattern for heterogeneous reactions.
12. Non-isothermal reactions.

## Course Outcomes

After taking this course, students should know how to implement reaction kinetics for the economical design of chemical reactors.

## List of Practicals

1. Determination of ionic conductivities using a conductivity cell and conductivity meter.
2. Obtain the reaction order with respect to ethyl acetate using the initial velocity method.
3. Obtain the reaction order with respect to sodium hydroxide using the initial velocity method.
4. Perform the integral method of analysis to find the reaction equation.
5. Perform the differential method of analysis to find the reaction equation.
6. Perform the half-life method of analysis to find the reaction equation.
7. Study the dependence of residence time.
8. Study the dependence of the speed constant and the conversion on temperature.
9. Discontinuous operation: mixture effects.
10. Discontinuous operation: velocity constant computation with constant sodium hydroxide initial concentration.
11. Study the variation of the kinetic constant with temperature (Arrhenius equation).
12. Compare theoretical and experimental conversion; deviation from ideality.
13. Calculate the theoretical and practical conversion of a tubular reactor.
