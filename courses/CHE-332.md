# CHE-332: Chemical Engineering Thermodynamics-II

| | |
|---|---|
| **Credit Hours** | 3-1 |
| **Prerequisites** | CHE-231: Chemical Engineering Thermodynamics I |

## Course Objectives

- To introduce concepts related to thermodynamics and fundamental property relations (FPRs).
- To introduce students to the main types of flow processes, their thermodynamics, and design from a thermodynamic-parameters point of view.
- To introduce the concepts of refrigeration processes, their thermodynamics, and application.
- To make students capable of understanding solution thermodynamics and its application.
- To make students capable of understanding vapor-liquid equilibrium (VLE).

## Course Contents

1. Fundamental equations for closed systems.
2. Production of power from heat: steam power plant, heat engines, various cycles, and turbines.
3. Liquefaction; refrigeration and air conditioning in various cycles.
4. Two-component systems: liquid-vapor equilibrium with its models — Raoult's law, Henry's law.
5. Helmholtz function.
6. Gibbs function (free energy) G; Maxwell's relationships.
7. Properties of mixtures of ideal gases; G for ideal and non-ideal gases.
8. Partial molar quantities.
9. Excess thermodynamic functions.
10. Chemical equilibrium: equilibrium constants for gas-phase reactions.
11. Factors affecting degree of conversion.
12. Equilibrium involving condensed phases; equilibrium in solution mixing.
13. Thermodynamics of cells.
14. Ideal and non-ideal solutions.
15. Composition of vapor in equilibrium with liquid; fractional distillation; azeotropes; liquid-solid equilibrium.
16. Compound formation; solid solutions.

## Course Outcomes

Students should be able to understand and apply the principles of thermodynamics to equilibrium calculations in multi-component and multiphase systems.

## List of Practicals

1. Estimate and compute power input, heat output, and coefficient of performance.
2. Interpret heat pump performance curves over a range of source and delivery temperatures.
3. Interpret the vapor compression cycle on a P-h diagram and perform an energy balance study, comparing it with literature.
4. Estimate and compute heat pump performance curves over a range of evaporation and condensation temperatures.
5. Predict and analyze the cooling cycle by compression of the vapor cycle of a heat pump.
6. Estimate and compute the steam pressure curve for a steam power plant.
7. Estimate and compute the fuel consumption rate and power supplied by the fuel.
8. Estimate and compute the amount of steam, steam power output, and boiler efficiency.
9. Estimate and compute the effect of compressor pressure ratio on volumetric efficiency.
10. Interpret and analyze adiabatic compression and expansion by performing the experiment slowly, in incremental steps.
11. Investigate, compute, and analyze adiabatic compression and expansion by performing the experiment fast, in incremental steps.
12. Estimate and describe the relation between pressure and temperature for the refrigeration cycle.
13. Estimate and analyze the effect of evaporation and condensation temperatures on the cooling rate and the heat transfer at the condenser.
