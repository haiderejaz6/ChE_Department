## Course Contents Overview

In reference to various international practices and the outcomes of the General Education Committee, NUST, the course curriculum has been redefined to be more comprehensive and engaging for students' understanding of Islam. The revised course content, teaching methodology, and mapping to General Education Attributes (GEAs) are outlined below.

## Teaching Plan

| Week | Topics | Teaching/Assessment Methodology | GEA(s) | Contact Hrs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Introduction to Islam:** Why the need for Divine Guidance? The purpose of life in this world. Importance of Islamic Studies in understanding Islam. | Inquiry-based discussions & Socratic circles: use open-ended questions to simulate hearing and discussion. | 1 | 2 |
| 2–4 | **Fundamental Theological Principles of Islam:** Tawheed and the existence of God; finality of Prophethood; self-accountability and the belief in the Hereafter; obligation of the major Ibadaat — a comprehensive framework for personal and social well-being. | Problem-based learning: develop purposeful-life projects balancing spiritual/material goals. | 2, 3 | 6 |
| 5–6 | **Introduction to Quran and Prophetic Hadith — the Divine Guidance:** Introduction to the Quran, its structure and composition; living by the Qur'an — etiquettes and daily application; introduction to Hadith — compilation, classification, and authenticity. | Inquiry-based discussion and problem-based learning. | 1 | 4 |
| 7–8 | **Sirat-un-Nabi (ﷺ): Ethical and Moral Lessons from His Blessed Life:** The perfection of his magnificent character and noble attributes; the perfection of his political and military leadership; the relation of the Ummah with the Holy Prophet (ﷺ). | Simulated hearings and case studies; readings in classical Muslim heritage. | 1–8 | 4 |
| 9 | Midterm Exam | — | — | — |
| 10–11 | **Family and Society in Islam:** Honoring and maintaining the family; etiquettes in interactions with the opposite gender in Islam; rights and responsibilities in protecting one's life, property, and honor; the role of Da'wah in Islam — promoting good and addressing social evils. | Role-play: simulate family conflict resolution or gender interaction scenarios. Case studies: analyze real-world examples of Silah Rahim in diverse cultures. | 6, 7 | 4 |
| 12–13 | **Ethical Framework of Islam:** Foundations of Islamic ethics — concept of right and wrong; Ihsan (excellence) as a core principle in Islamic ethics, including tolerance, forgiveness, keeping one's promises, and Tazkiya (self-purification); digital ethics — Islamic guidelines for social media engagement. | Project-based learning: design an "Ethics Handbook" for social media or workplaces. Case studies: evaluate ethical dilemmas in tech/business from an Islamic perspective. | 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 | 4 |
| 14 | **Islamic Jurisprudence:** Fundamentals of Islamic jurisprudence; Ijtihad — its significance and relevance in the modern era; introduction to Islamic trade and finance, its stability, and regulations in different fields of life (engineering, economics, etc.). | Case studies: analyze Islamic finance structures and contract disputes. Role-play: simulate a mock Ijtihad council addressing modern dilemmas. | 7 | 2 |
| 15 | **Islamic Civilization — Prominent Features:** Characteristics of Muslim civilization and the Rashidun Caliphate; Muslim innovations in science and technology. | Group presentations: analyze landmark institutions (e.g., the House of Wisdom) to trace how specific innovations emerged and spread. | 8 | 2 |
| 16–17 | **Islam and the Modern World:** Relevance of Islam in the modern world (challenges and prospects); Islamophobia, interfaith dialogue, and multiculturalism; Islamic responses to social, ethical, and technological changes. | Inquiry-based discussions: debate Islam's relevance in a globalized world. Group presentations: propose solutions to challenges faced by Muslims. | 8 | 4 |
| 18 | End Semester Exam | — | — | — |
