# CHE-346: Particulate Technology

| | |
|---|---|
| **Credit Hours** | 3-1 |
| **Prerequisites** | None |

## Course Objectives

- To enable students to understand the basic principles of handling solid particles and unit operations related to particles, such as crushing, screening, and mixing.
- To cover the basic principles of crystallization and other separation techniques.

## Course Contents

1. Characterization of solid particles: particle size, particle size distribution, and shape of solid particles; particle size analysis.
2. Storage and conveying of solid particles: bulk storage, bin storage, flow out of bins, pneumatic and hydraulic conveying, belt conveyors and elevators.
3. Mixing of solids: mixer performance, mixers for cohesive solids, mixers for non-cohesive solids.
4. Size reduction: methods of size reduction, characteristics of comminuted products, energy and power requirement in comminution.
5. Machinery for size reduction: crushers, grinders, ultrafine grinders, cutting machines.
6. Size enlargement: agglomeration phenomena and its applications (e.g., granulation, pelletization, tabling) and storage; dust explosion.
7. Mechanical separation: screening, screening equipment, stationary screens, gyrating screens, vibrating screens, ideal and actual screens.
8. Solid-liquid and gas-solid separation: cyclone separators, electrostatic separators.
9. Filtration: types of filtration equipment, classification of filters, filtration theory.
10. Gravity settling processes: gravity classifiers, sorting classifiers, equipment for sedimentation.

## Course Outcomes

After completing this course, students must know the basic principles of handling solid particles and unit operations related to chemical engineering plant design.

## List of Practicals

1. Compute the abrasion value of copper ore and brick samples by operating a jaw crusher. Analyze experimental results graphically and compare findings with literature.
2. Estimate the average diameters of crushed particles by operating a jaw crusher and sieve shaker, and plot the experimental results graphically.
3. Compute the individual and overall critical speed of a ball mill using different sizes of grinding media and compare results with literature.
4. Investigate the effect of RPM on particle size of the sample operating the ball mill at constant grinding media size. Analyze the findings graphically.
5. Compute the percentage of magnetic particles for the unknown sample and predict the efficiency of a magnetic separator. Compare experimental results with literature.
6. Operate the magnetic separator at different RPM and interpret the experimental results graphically for different samples.
7. Synthesize NaCl crystals in batch crystallization and investigate the effect of cooling in an agitated tank crystallizer. Analyze the experimental results graphically.
8. Perform FTIR analysis of synthesized NaCl crystal to estimate the purity of the sample. Compare experimental results with literature.
9. Predict the particle size distribution of the product obtained from a jaw crusher and analyze the effect of mesh number on particle size graphically.
10. Compute the particle size distribution of the product obtained from a ball mill and interpret the effect of mesh number on particle size graphically.
11. Identify the unknown sample by computing its permeability using distilled water as the mother liquor. Analyze the findings graphically.
12. Predict the filtration rate of calcium carbonate solution by operating the plate and frame filter press assembly at constant feed rate. Analyze the findings graphically.
13. Compute the concentration of titanium oxide and calcium carbonate solution by operating a centrifuge at constant RPM. Analyze the experimental results graphically.
